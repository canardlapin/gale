# Consuming and row-scaled QR admission

This receipt evaluates three algebraic ownership boundaries at the exact
regress4s M5 shape `n=1024,p=5,q=8`: consuming a transient matrix builder as
QR's working factor buffer, constructing `diag(scales) * A` directly in that
buffer, and filling `diag(scales) * B` directly into reusable solve scratch.

The source-pinned baseline is commit
`df94c13725ceb4c8b3a27dd9e5c11d618285547b`; its only issue-scoped change is
the benchmark class containing the three materialized controls. The candidate
uses that commit plus this bead's implementation and direct benchmark routes.
Raw receipts are the [baseline](./2026-08-03-scaled-qr-baseline.json) and
[candidate](./2026-08-03-scaled-qr-candidate.json).

## Environment and protocol

The court ran on a MacBook Pro with an Apple M3 Max (14 cores, 36 GB), macOS
14.3 arm64, JDK 25.0.1, sbt 1.11.7, Scala 3.7.4, and JMH 1.37. It used one
thread, two forks, five 500 ms warmups, ten 500 ms measurements, average time
in microseconds, and the GC profiler. Inputs are immutable outside the timed
boundary; builder construction/fill, algebraic scaling, QR, solve work, and
owned results are timed. Candidate workspaces are pre-sized and reused.

```text
sbt 'benchmarksJVM/Jmh/run -f 2 -wi 5 -i 10 -w 500ms -r 500ms -t 1 -prof gc -rf json -rff /absolute/path/candidate.json gale.bench.ScaledQrConstructionJmh.*'
```

## Results

| comparison | baseline us/op | direct us/op | change | baseline B/op | direct B/op | bytes removed |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| builder result then QR vs consume QR | 60.212 | 53.971 | -10.36% | 131,603.271 | 82,321.271 | 49,282.000 |
| materialized scaled QR vs direct scaled QR | 59.883 | 54.571 | -8.87% | 131,603.300 | 82,320.759 | 49,282.541 |
| materialized scaled solve vs direct scaled solve | 93.451 | 78.326 | -16.18% | 263,217.297 | 82,777.208 | 180,440.089 |

The builder route removes more than the required `8*m*p = 40,960` payload
bytes because its explicit workspace is also reused. Direct scaled QR retains
only QR's required owned factor storage and removes the temporary scaled matrix.
The direct solve additionally removes the `1024x8` scaled response and its
second transformed-RHS copy; its remaining allocation is the QR factor and
owned `5x8` coefficients.

Candidate materialized controls preserved allocation. Their runtimes were
59.145 us/op for builder-result-then-QR and 59.187 us/op for scaled QR. The
candidate materialized solve mean was distorted by one 150.713 us/op GC
outlier; its allocation remained exactly 263,217.337 B/op, so the source-pinned
93.451 us/op court is the stable timing comparison.

## Numerical and ownership contract

`DMatBuilder.consumeQR` closes the builder before transferring its array; every
later read, write, fill, result, or second consumption fails. QR owns the
transferred factor buffer and never exposes a mutable alias. Direct row scaling
is deliberately algebraic rather than weight-specific: finite zero and negative
scales are supported exactly as multiplication, while non-finite scales and
length mismatches return typed errors.

Differential tests require exact factor entries, pivot permutations, ranks,
solves, and covariance against result-then-QR and explicitly materialized
scaling. They cover strided matrices/vectors, zero and negative scales, rank
changes, extreme finite magnitudes, vector and matrix RHS inputs, closed-builder
failures, result ownership, and repeated workspace reuse on JVM and Scala.js.
